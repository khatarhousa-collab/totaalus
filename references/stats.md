# Stats

Modelled on: https://www.odido.nl/blog/

---

## The rule

Numbers make claims real. Every time you make a promise or a comparison, anchor it with a specific number. Never let a number stand alone — always pair it with the benefit it proves.

---

## How Odido does it

They don't dump a table of specs. They weave numbers into sentences naturally, always in service of the reader's decision.

**Number + benefit in one sentence:**
> "Werkt in meer dan 140 landen, vanaf € 2,50 per dag."

The number (140 landen) proves coverage. The price (€ 2,50) proves affordability. One sentence, two trust signals.

**Specific beats vague every time:**
- "99,9% uptime" beats "bijna altijd online"
- "binnen 5 minuten" beats "snel"
- "80.000+ zenders" beats "duizenden zenders"
- "15 dagen geld-terug" beats "ruime garantie"

## How to use stats in a sentence

**Pattern:** [Stat] + [what it means for the reader]
> "IPTVTotaal biedt 80.000+ actieve zenders — inclusief alle Nederlandse publieke en commerciële kanalen."

**Pattern:** [Comparison] + [stat proves the winner]
> "Netflix kost tot €22,99 per maand. IPTVTotaal al vanaf €4,60."

**Pattern:** [Requirement] + [stat reassures]
> "Voor 4K heb je minimaal 25 Mbps nodig. De meeste Nederlandse internetabonnementen halen dat ruimschoots."

---

## What to avoid

- Don't use vague numbers: ~~"honderden zenders"~~, ~~"vele aanbieders"~~
- Don't cite stats without a source if they're industry claims (speeds, market data)
- Don't stack more than 2–3 numbers in a single paragraph — it kills readability
