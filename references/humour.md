# Humour

Modelled on: https://www.odido.nl/blog/

---

## The rule

Never try to be funny. Be relatable. The humour comes from naming a frustration so accurately that the reader thinks "yes, exactly that."

Odido's humour is dry, brief, and situational. It never announces itself. No emoji, no "haha", no winking — just a sentence that lands.

---

## How it works in practice

**Name the frustration casually:**
> "Je hoeft op een buitenlands vliegveld niet in de rij te staan voor een tijdelijke fysieke simkaart."

Nobody says "this is funny." But everyone who has stood in that queue at an airport recognises it instantly. That recognition IS the humour.

**Use the short deadpan follow-up:**
A longer sentence sets up a mild frustration or complexity — then a very short sentence deflates it.

> "De eSIM is een digitale sim die is ingebouwd in je telefoon. Je kan 'm er niet uithalen. Dat hoeft ook niet."

"Dat hoeft ook niet." — three words. That's the joke. It reassures AND lightly pokes fun at the absurdity of the concern.

**Understatement over overstatement:**
> "Geen gedoe met kabels."

Not: "Je hoeft je nooit meer zorgen te maken over vervelende, ingewikkelde bekabeling!" — that's trying too hard.

---

## Templates to steal

- "[Frustrating scenario]? Dat hoeft niet meer."
- "Geen gedoe met [thing]."
- "[Short setup]. [Shorter punchline that reframes it]."
- Name the exact situation readers hate — airport queues, buffering during the final, installation screens nobody understands

---

## What NOT to do

- No puns
- No forced references to memes or trends
- No laughing at the reader
- No sarcasm — it reads as rude in Dutch blog writing
- Never more than one humorous beat per section — treat it like salt, not sugar
