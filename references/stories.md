# Stories

Modelled on: https://www.odido.nl/blog/

---

## The rule

Odido doesn't write long narratives. They write micro-scenarios — one or two sentences that put the reader inside a situation they recognise. The "story" is just a moment. But it's the reader's moment.

---

## How Odido does it

**The situation opener:**
Start the article or a section by placing the reader in a familiar situation — before the solution exists.

> "Vakantie gepland buiten de Europese Unie? Je normale bundels gelden dan niet."

Two sentences. The reader is on holiday. Something is wrong. Now they need what comes next.

**The frustration scene:**
Name a specific, relatable pain point as if describing a movie scene the reader has lived through.

> "Je hoeft op een buitenlands vliegveld niet in de rij te staan voor een tijdelijke fysieke simkaart."

This works because the reader HAS stood in that queue. Or they can easily imagine it. The story isn't told — it's triggered.

**The before/after:**
Briefly sketch life before the product, then life after. Keep it to 2–3 sentences total.

> "Waar je vroeger dure losse sport-abonnementen nodig had, geeft IPTVTotaal je toegang tot alle grote sportzenders in één pakket."

One sentence covers the entire before/after arc. No drama needed.

---

## Story-opening templates

Use these to open a blog post or a section:

- "Stel je voor: [relatable situation]. [Problem that follows]. [Solution introduced]."
- "[Frustrating thing that happens to everyone]? Dat hoeft niet meer."
- "Je zit op de bank. [Specific moment]. Dan wil je gewoon [thing that works]."
- "Vroeger moest je [complicated old way]. Nu is het anders."
- "[Question the reader is asking themselves right now]."

---

## What to avoid

- No long anecdotes — if a story takes more than 3 sentences, cut it in half
- No fictional characters ("Stel, Jan wil graag...") — speak directly to the reader
- No inspirational arcs — this is tech, not a TED talk
- Don't open every post with a story — sometimes a direct statement is stronger
