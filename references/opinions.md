# Opinions

Modelled on: https://www.odido.nl/blog/

---

## The rule

State opinions clearly but without aggression. Sound like a friend giving a recommendation, not a brand pushing a product. Confident, not desperate.

---

## How Odido does it

They don't hedge with "some people might say..." or "it could be argued that...". They just say what they think — gently but directly.

**Direct recommendation:**
> "Een eSIM is een goede optie."

Not: "Een eSIM kan in sommige gevallen een interessante mogelijkheid zijn om te overwegen."

**Clear ranking without being dismissive:**
> "TiviMate is de meest populaire keuze onder Android-gebruikers. IPTV Smarters Pro is een goed gratis alternatief."

They rank. They pick winners. But they acknowledge alternatives exist.

**Positioning without trash-talking competitors:**
Odido never says "competitor X is bad." They say "here's why this option is better for you." Keep the focus on the reader's benefit.

---

## Opinion phrases that work

- "Dit is waar [X] écht schittert."
- "De beste keuze voor jou hangt af van [Y]."
- "Als je [use case], dan is [X] de slimste keuze."
- "Eerlijk gezegd: [direct take]."
- "[X] is prima. Maar als je ook [Y] wilt, dan win je meer met [Z]."

---

## When to give an opinion

- When comparing two options (IPTV vs Netflix, 4K vs HD, cable vs wifi)
- When recommending an app or device
- When the reader has a clear problem to solve

## When to hold back

- Don't editorialize about things the reader hasn't asked about
- Don't volunteer opinions on price unless you're making a comparison
- Never sound like you're trying to convince someone — sound like you're informing them and letting them decide
